Overview

What is dicyanin?

Dicyanin is a synthetic carbocyanine dye — two rings linked by a conjugated carbon chain. Typical form: iodide salt (~C25H25IN2).

Built as a photographic sensitizer so plates could respond to red and near-infrared, not only blue. Kilner later used alcoholic solutions in sealed glass cells (1911).

Look

Dark green crystals; deep blue-green solutions.

Then

Red / near-IR plate sensitization.

Kilner

Alcoholic solutions in glass screens, 1911.

Now

Rare, toxic, unstable.

Critical

Safety

Not a home project

Dicyanin is toxic. Old routes use ethyl iodide, sodium sulfide, chloroform, strong bases.

Lab only: fume hood, training, PPE. Historical documentation ≠ permission to experiment casually.

Main hazards

  • Toxic dye dust / residues
  • Flammable solvents (ethanol, ether)
  • Chloroform vapor · sulfide solutions
  • Eye strain from dense filters

Context

Myth vs record

On the record

  • Dicyanin was a real photographic sensitizer
  • Synthesis papers exist (e.g. 1920, 1923)
  • Kilner published screen methods in 1911
  • Strong colored filters change what you see

Not established

  • That screens reveal a biological “aura”
  • Medical diagnosis via Kilner screens
  • That modern “dicyanin glasses” are authentic cells
  • That this chemistry is safe for home use

Afterimages, contrast shifts, and expectation explain most aura reports without new physics.

Science

Properties

  • Solid: dark green, often metallic
  • Solution: deep blue-green to violet
  • Absorption: deep red (historically ~670 nm in some preps)
  • Stability: poor in solution — cool, dark, fresh
  • Screen solvent: ethanol

Compared with other sensitizers

Make the dye

Historical Palkin-style outline. Not home chemistry.

Advanced / toxicHistorical synthesis4 steps
Step 1 of 4
1

Ethiodide intermediate

Base + roughly equal weight ethyl iodide. Warm until solid. Wash ether. Recrystallize from boiling 95% ethanol. Dry.

2

Alcoholic sodium sulfide

~25 g Na2S·9H2O boiled ~2 min in 100 mL 95% ethanol. Cool, filter.

3

Condensation

~1 g ethiodide / 25 mL solution. ~1.5 mL chloroform per gram intermediate. ~50°C, 30 min. Cool — dye precipitates.

4

Isolate

Filter. Wash ethanol → ether → water → ethanol/ether. Dry dark. Dark green crystalline solid.

Materials

Screen materials

For a Kilner-style cell

  • Dicyanin powder (toxic, scarce)
  • High-purity ethanol
  • Two optical glass plates
  • Spacers ~0.1–0.5 mm
  • Alcohol-compatible sealant
  • Pipette, dark storage, PPE

Modern “aura glasses” are usually ordinary filters, not sealed dye cells.

Terms

Glossary

Carbocyanine

Two nitrogen heterocycles linked by a conjugated polymethine chain.

Sensitizer

Dye that extends the wavelengths an emulsion responds to.

Kilner screen

Sealed glass cell with dilute alcoholic dye solution (1911 pattern).

Ethiodide

Here: quaternary salt intermediate from the quinoline base + ethyl iodide.

FAQ

Questions

Still manufactured?

Not in any ordinary commercial sense.

Real aura?

No solid scientific evidence. Filters + expectation suffice.

Why photography?

Early plates were weak in the red; dyes extended the spectrum.

Modern “dicyanin glasses”?

Usually ordinary colored filters, not historical dye cells.

Safe at home?

Synthesis: no. Casual handling of authentic dye: no.
